# Cross-National Differences in Teacher Burnout

## Abstract

This study compares teacher burnout across five countries using the **Maslach Burnout Inventory**.

## Results

### Descriptive Statistics

| Country | N | EE Mean (SD) | DP Mean (SD) | PA Mean (SD) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sweden | 412 | 22.1 (8.3) | 5.8 (4.2) | 38.2 (6.1) |
| Turkey | 387 | 28.4 (9.1) | 8.2 (5.7) | 32.1 (7.4) |
| Japan | 298 | 31.2 (7.8) | 6.1 (3.9) | 29.8 (8.2) |
| Brazil | 456 | 26.7 (10.2) | 7.4 (5.1) | 34.5 (6.8) |
| India | 523 | 24.3 (8.9) | 6.9 (4.8) | 36.1 (5.9) |

### Regression Analysis

| Predictor | β | SE | t | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workload | 0.42 | 0.05 | 8.40 | <.001 |
| Autonomy | −0.28 | 0.04 | −7.00 | <.001 |
| Support | −0.19 | 0.06 | −3.17 | .002 |
| Experience | −0.11 | 0.03 | −3.67 | <.001 |

Note: EE = Emotional Exhaustion; DP = Depersonalization; PA = Personal Accomplishment.

## Discussion

Japanese teachers reported the highest emotional exhaustion, consistent with findings on *karōshi* culture and expectations of long working hours beyond contractual obligations.
