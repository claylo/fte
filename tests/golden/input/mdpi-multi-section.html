Introduction

Titanium alloys, particularly Ti-6Al-4V, are the gold standard for load-bearing biomedical implants due to their excellent biocompatibility, corrosion resistance, and mechanical properties.

Additive Manufacturing Processes

Powder Bed Fusion

Selective Laser Melting (SLM)

SLM uses a focused laser beam to selectively melt metallic powder layer by layer. Key process parameters include:

  • Laser power (150–400 W)
  • Scan speed (500–1500 mm/s)
  • Layer thickness (20–50 μm)
  • Hatch spacing (60–120 μm)

Electron Beam Melting (EBM)

EBM operates under vacuum using an electron beam, producing parts with lower residual stress than SLM due to the elevated build temperature (>600°C).

Directed Energy Deposition

DED processes feed powder or wire into a melt pool created by a laser or electron beam, enabling repair and functionally graded structures.

Surface Modification

Post-processing treatments improve biocompatibility:

  1. Anodization — creates a TiO2 nanotube layer promoting osseointegration
  2. Hydroxyapatite coating — mimics bone mineral composition
  3. Chemical etching — produces micro-roughness favorable for cell adhesion

Conclusions

Additive manufacturing enables patient-specific implant geometries with controlled porosity, but fatigue performance of as-built parts remains inferior to wrought material. Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) closes this gap substantially.
