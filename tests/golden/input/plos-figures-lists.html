Abstract

We tracked 47 Arctic terns (Sterna paradisaea) from breeding colonies in Greenland and Iceland using light-level geolocators.

Introduction

The Arctic tern undertakes the longest known annual migration of any animal, traveling from Arctic breeding grounds to Antarctic waters and back — a round trip exceeding 70,000 km.

Methods

Geolocator Deployment

Miniaturized geolocators (0.65 g, <1% body mass) were attached to leg bands.

Data Processing

Positions were derived using the threshold method with the following steps:

  1. Light curves were inspected for shading events
  2. Sunrise and sunset times were identified using a light threshold of 2 lux
  3. Latitude was estimated from day length and longitude from the time of solar noon
  4. An iterative smoothing algorithm removed outliers

Results

Stopover Sites

Five key staging areas were identified:

  • North Atlantic — Newfoundland Banks (Aug–Sep)
  • Eastern Atlantic — Canary Current upwelling (Sep–Oct)
  • Southern Africa — Benguela Current (Oct–Nov)
  • Indian Ocean — south of Madagascar (Nov–Dec)
  • Southern Ocean — Weddell Sea pack ice edge (Dec–Mar)

Discussion

The spring return migration was significantly faster than the autumn departure, with birds exploiting prevailing westerly winds across the Southern Ocean.

These birds experience two summers per year, and thus more daylight than any other creature on the planet.
